# V027 Research — Civic places, Pittsburgh / Mon Valley (1850–1990s)

Map bounds: lat 40.2827–40.4772, lon -80.0824 – -79.8280. Items outside are marked **OFFMAP**.

Coordinate method key (in the `coord src` column):
- **NRHP** = coordinates from the Wikipedia NRHP listings tables (Pittsburgh / Allegheny County), which come from the NRHP nomination.
- **WP** = coordinates from the article's own Wikipedia infobox.
- **HMDB/PHMC** = from a historical-marker record (hmdb.org or the Wikipedia list of PA state historical markers).
- **FaG** = Find a Grave cemetery GPS.
- **geo** = I geocoded a stated historic address or corner by hand, using nearby NRHP points as reference. Treat these as ±100–200 m. Confidence is capped at med, and is usually low.

Short source keys used below:
- NRHP-P = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania
- NRHP-AC = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Allegheny_County,_Pennsylvania
- PHLF = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pittsburgh_History_and_Landmarks_Foundation_Historic_Landmarks
- CITY = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_City_of_Pittsburgh_historic_designations
- PHMC = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pennsylvania_state_historical_markers_in_Allegheny_County

## Notes and conflicts

1. **Arsenal victims in the Allegheny Cemetery mass grave:** WP says 54 unidentified; battlefields.org says "about 40"; the monument text says 45. The total dead (78) is consistent across sources.
2. **Western Penitentiary, Woods Run opening:** the WP infobox says 1882, but its text says inmates were received from 1879. Use 1882 for "official", 1879 for first use.
3. **St. Paul's Orphan Asylum founding:** 1836 (rootsweb, Sisters of Charity) vs 1838 (Pittsburgh Quarterly, Sisters of St. Joseph).
4. **Protestant Orphan Asylum:** the Pittsburgh Quarterly "Protestant Home" (1832, First Presbyterian) and the "Orphan Asylum of Pittsburgh & Allegheny" (Society 1832, building 1837–38 on N Taylor Ave) are almost certainly the same body. I treated them as one item.
5. **Hazelwood library:** the Carnegie list says opened 15 Aug 1900; the WP branch article says 1899. **South Side library:** 30 Jan 1908 (list) vs 1909 (PHLF).
6. **Kingsley House move to the Hill:** 1900 (Heinz) vs 1901 (WP).
7. **St. Anthony's Chapel:** WP says built 1882, dedicated 1883, enlarged 1892; PHLF says 1880 and 1891.
8. **Kaufmann's building years:** WP gives the 1887 flagship, the 1913 expansion and clock, and the "by 1916" addition; PHLF lists 1898 and 1913. The 1913 clock is solid.
9. **1897 fire:** Firehouse.com gives 2 May 1897 (just after midnight), starting at T.C. Jenkins & Co. and spreading to Horne's, 19 buildings, 1 firefighter killed. The brief's "May 3" is probably the newspaper date.
10. **Homewood Cemetery acreage:** I used ~175 acres at founding. The WP infobox figure for the current size was much larger and looks inconsistent with the cemetery's actual size, so I didn't use it.
11. **"Monongahela Cemetery":** the one with a Wikipedia article is in Monongahela city (Washington Co., OFFMAP). The Mon Valley one inside the map is in North Braddock (1111 Fourth St).
12. **Allegheny City fire and police unification** with Pittsburgh is assumed to follow the 1907 annexation. I found no direct source.
13. **"geo" coordinates** are hand-geocoded from the stated address relative to nearby NRHP points, and should be checked in a map before placing sprites precisely. They are all marked low or med.
14. **Bethel AME** has two sites: the downtown 1st Ave/Smithfield site (PHMC marker) and the Wylie & Elm Lower Hill church, demolished 24 Jul 1957. Its intermediate moves were not traced.
15. **First courthouse on the Diamond:** used until 1836 and sold 11 Aug 1841. Don't confuse this with the 1841 Grant St courthouse (Timeline). The current courthouse dates from 1888.
16. **Several brief items are slightly OFFMAP on the north edge (lat > 40.4772):** McCleary School, Highland Reservoir No. 1, Brilliant pumping station, the Aspinwall plant and St. Stanislaus cemetery. St. Nicholas Millvale (40.4770) sits right on the boundary.
