Control technology and cost
- Electrostatic precipitator: Cottrell patent 1907; licensed widely after 1912
- Baghouses, BOF hoods, coke-oven door seals, quench-water treatment, scrubbers: mostly 1960s–70s [U dates]
- Steel industry, 1970s: ~$8.5B of control equipment (>15% of capital), +$27/ton on ~$400/ton steel, $2.3B/yr to run
- Battelle 1979 cost per kg of steel by share of pollutants removed: 90% $0.26, 97% $4.98, 99% $32.20
- OTA 1980: build new processes (direct reduction) rather than retrofit old plants

## 5. Globalisation and the collapse (steel and the other industries)

Demand and capacity (US)
- Finished-steel shipments: 111M short tons 1973, >100M 1979, 62M 1982, 68M 1983, 73M 1984; apparent consumption
  1984 ~93M, ~20% below 1979. Raw steel Q3 1973 28M vs Q3 1982 14M; 1982 output −40.1% (largest fall since 1947)
- Capacity peak 160M tons 1977 → 136M 1985; integrated 138M (1980) → 90M (1987)
- Utilisation 92% 1974, 91% 1979, 48–49% 1982, 65% Feb 1985; break-even ~70%; 1982 big seven lost $95/t at $412/t
- US share of world steel 26% 1964 → 17% 1979 → 12% 1984
- Steel per car 3,300 lb (1978) → 2,300 lb (1984); autos 21% → 17% of shipments; aluminium cans from 1965
- Steel jobs: 650,000 (1953), 512,000 (1974), 399,000 (1980), 236,000 (1984); USS 75,000 → 20,000 (1980–88)
- Mon Valley ~90,000 in basic steel at peak; J&L ~10,000 around Pittsburgh; Pennsylvania share of US raw steel 24%
  (1965) → 15% (1983) while Indiana rose 13% → 24%

Imports (share of apparent consumption)
- 1950s ~2%; net importer from the 116-day 1959 strike; 1965 10%; 1970 13.8; 1971 17.9; 1972 16.6; 1973 12.4;
  1974 13.4; 1975 13.5; 1976 14.1; 1977 17.8; 1978 18.1; 1979 15.2; 1980 16.3; 1981 18.9; 1982 21.8; 1983 20.5;
  1984 26.4 (26.2M t); 1985 25.2
- Sources 1976: Japan ~56%, EC ~22%; 1982 US–EC deal caps EC at 5.5% of US consumption; 1984 VRA Japan 6%

Prices and costs
- April 1962: USS +$6/t withdrawn after Kennedy's press conference
- US producer price $149/t (1970), $365 (1979), $412 (1981), $361 (1985); Antwerp export $249 (1973), $382 (1980),
  $273 (1985); US ~$93/t above Antwerp 1984–85
- 1977 Japan ~$73/t (25%) cheaper on finished product; freight, insurance and duty $44–54/t
- Hourly cost $3.30 (1956) → $25.20 (1982) with productivity +5.5%; unit labour cost $265/t US vs $144/t Japan
  (1982); 1983 hourly US >$22, Japan/Germany/France ~$12, UK ~$8; steel wage premium over manufacturing 28% (1969)
  → 64% (1981–82)
- BOF: US first 1954 (McLouth), USS/Bethlehem 1964; Japan ~80% BOF by 1970; US 1984 BOF 58%, electric 33%, open
  hearth 9%. Continuous casting 1982: Japan 71%, EC 45%, US 21%; 1984 US 38%, Japan ~85%
- 1980: 10.1 man-hours per finished ton (US) vs ~0.5 at some minimills

Trade policy
- VRAs 1969–74 (renewed 1972 with UK; lapsed in the 1974 shortage): prices +1.2–3.5%
- Sept 1977 antidumping cases; Trigger Price Mechanism Feb 1978 (Solomon Report): import prices up to +10%, domestic
  +1%, imports 21.2M t (1978) → 17.5M (1979); suspended Mar 1980, back Oct 1980, ended 1982 (110 petitions)
- Oct 1982 US–EC arrangement; July 1983 specialty-steel tariffs and quotas
- Sept 1984 VRAs: 18.5% finished steel + 1.5M t semi-finished, 1985–89; Steel Import Stabilization Act (1 Oct 1984 –
  30 Sep 1989), conditioned on reinvestment; ~$3.5B a year to users

Macro
- 1974 boom (92% utilisation, 17% return on equity), 1975 recession; 1979 boom; 1980 and 1981–82 recessions
- Trade-weighted dollar +77% mid-1980 → Feb 1985 (imports ~45% cheaper)

Minimills
- ~10M t capacity 1975, ~25M 1987; >15% of output 1983, ~16% 1985; Nucor 100% continuous casting, 2 man-hours vs 6–8

Other industries
- Westinghouse: appliances sold 1974; turbine HQ to Orlando 1982–83; East Pittsburgh and Lester closed 1988
- PPG: first US float glass 1963; last plate line 1973; outmoded plants closed 1975; sales $1B (1968), $2B (1976)
- Mesta: bankrupt Feb 1983, West Homestead sold June 1983; Dravo Neville Island yard closed 1982 (16,000 in WWII)
- Aluminium: LME trading from Oct 1978; ingot $2,120/t (1980) → $950 (1982) → $1,650 (1983) → $1,040 (1985); ten
  US smelters closed in the 1980s
- Clairton: ~7,000 (late 1970s) → 4,800 (1983) → ~1,100 (end 1985); survived

Pittsburgh closures and jobs
- Carrie Furnaces offline 1978; J&L Pittsburgh properties to Park Corp 1981; Dravo 1982; Mesta 1983; Duquesne
  announced 28 Dec 1983, closed 1984; LTV South Side and Homestead 1986; National Tube 29 Aug 1987; East Pittsburgh 1988
- Pittsburgh MSA unemployment >17% Jan 1983 (15.7% for 1983); Beaver ~27%, Fayette ~25%, Allegheny ~14%; MSA lost
  78,000 jobs in the early-1980s recessions; region lost >125,000 manufacturing jobs in the 1980s; population −7%
- Survivors: Edgar Thomson, Irvin, Clairton (Mon Valley Works); ET's caster only in 1992
